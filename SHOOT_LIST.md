# Shot list

Ordered by what the site needs most. Phone camera is fine for all of it — a
recent iPhone or Pixel shooting in good light beats a borrowed DSLR used badly.

**Send the original camera-roll files, not Instagram exports.** Instagram
re-encodes to roughly 1080px and throws away most of the detail. The original is
usually 3–4× larger and it is the single cheapest quality win available.

**Anyone recognisable has to say yes** before their photo goes on the site. A
shot taken from behind avoids the question entirely and is often the better
photograph anyway — it shows the line-up and the fade, which is what people are
actually paying for.

---

## 1. ⭐ The shop, lit, at 22:00

**The most valuable missing asset. It is the brand.**

The shopfront from across Klapdorp, after dark, with the lights on inside and the
FADED wall catching them. Shoot it in the real conditions the site describes: a
Wednesday or Thursday around 21:30–22:00, when you are the only lit window on the
street.

- From across the road, whole facade in frame, straight on
- Then a second from an angle, so the street runs away behind it
- Horizontal, held steady, no flash — let the window do the work
- One vertical version of each for phones

The site currently uses a daytime photo of the same wall at 1440px. A proper
night frame would replace it and would be the hero of the whole page.

## 2. The FADED wall itself, in daylight, at full resolution

The existing photo is good and too small. Same wall, same framing, original file
straight off the camera. Flat light, no strong shadow across the letters.

## 3. Alabi at work

Hands and clippers, mid-cut, from the side. Face optional — the work is the
subject. Three or four frames:

- Clippers against the neckline
- The comb-and-scissor grip, close
- Standing back to check the shape
- One portrait, looking at camera, if you want one on the site

## 4. The chair, the mirror, the room

Empty. No client, no consent question, and an empty chair under a warm light at
night is a strong photograph on its own.

- The chair, straight on, room behind it
- The mirror and the counter with the tools laid out
- A detail: clippers, combs, the brush — flat lay on the counter

## 5. The work — with permission

This is what fills the gallery the site does not currently have. For each cut:

- **From behind**, close, showing the fade gradient and the neckline. This is the
  most useful shot on the list and needs no one's face.
- Three-quarter from the side, showing the shape
- Straight-on only where the client has agreed

Aim for a spread of hair types and lengths — the whole pricing argument is that
the ladder runs by length and texture, not by gender, and the photographs should
show that. **Textured and coily hair especially**: fade work on it is the hardest
thing to photograph well and the most convincing when it is done right.

Good light, no flash. Stand where the window light hits, or under the brightest
lamp, and shoot from about a metre and a half.

## 6. Two shots already in hand — get consent

The website's media library already holds a 20-second video and a portrait of the
same client with a fresh mid-length cut. Both are good. Neither is on the site,
because there is no record of that client agreeing to appear on it.

**One message to them turns both into publishable assets today.**
